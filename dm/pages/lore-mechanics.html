LORE & MECHANICS

Oathbound Compulsion

Every operative — Obsidian and Gray alike — bears two sigils implanted at birth, one on each hand, binding them to obey the direct spoken orders of a designated superior (in this one-shot, the Gold commander).

Trigger: a PC takes an action that directly defies an order given by their bound superior.

Each PC can break the oath twice per encounter — one sigil per break

  • 1st break (right-hand sigil): 2d6 damage (Constitution save, DC 14, for half) + disadvantage on the next roll made immediately after
  • 2nd break (left-hand sigil): 2d6 damage (Constitution save, DC 14, for half) + disadvantage on the next roll + 1 level of exhaustion
  • After both sigils are broken: the PC is fully free of the compulsion for the rest of the encounter — further orders have no mechanical effect on them

The compulsion is passive and always active — it doesn't require an action to resist, it simply triggers the moment defiance occurs. Players should know their oath is watching before Act 3, ideally reinforced narratively in Act 1 (a beat where an Obsidian visibly can't act against an order, or flinches when tempted to). Consider narrating the sigils visibly cracking/burning out on each break for a strong table moment.

Discovery Handout — The Logbook

  • Infernal-language ledger found near the Chimera Codex, recording every use: Color, date, requesting House, soul-cost paid to devil brokers
  • Entries show low Colors trading their own souls, and both low and high Colors trading the souls of their slaves
  • One entry ties to a Gold House sharing the strike team's Gold's family name — his cousin
  • Discoverable via Investigation/stealth or a social check (Deception/Insight) against a cagey archivist/guard

Caste-to-Class Mapping

Caste is a social/legal tier, not a species restriction — any race can belong to any Color. Caste determines training, starting gear, and legal access to magic. Only Gray and Obsidian are playable castes in this one-shot — Gold is reserved for Servian as an NPC commander.

Gold (NPC-only in this one-shot)

Bred and trained to rule and command.

  • Paladin (Oath of Conquest/Glory fits the Society's ethos well; a defecting Gold could flip to Oath of Redemption)
  • Fighter (Eldritch Knight) — battlefield commanders who blend martial and arcane
  • Bard (College of Eloquence) — political/administrative Golds
  • Wizard — the "Peerless Scarred" intellectual/strategist track

Obsidian

Bred for war, raised for loyalty, bonded to a Gold house.

  • Barbarian — raw frontline power, Rage as controlled savagery
  • Fighter (Battle Master or Champion) — disciplined shock troops
  • Sorcerer — rare genetic anomaly, traced to illicit Gold-Obsidian bloodline mixing; the Society treats these as flaws or threats, not gifts

Thematic note: Obsidians are the most affected by the Oathbound sigils narratively — their whole upbringing is obedience, so breaking it should feel like the biggest personal violation at the table.

Gray

The Society's soldiers, police, and special forces.

  • Rogue (Soldier or Inquisitive-flavored) — infiltration, small-unit tactics
  • Ranger — battlefield scouts, survivalists
  • Fighter — standard-issue soldiers
  • Warlock — a quiet pact made with a devil broker; mirrors the exact crime the party investigates

Thematic note: Grays are trained to question and observe — which is exactly why the Rising agent is a Gray.

Magic Access by Caste

Magic in this setting is treated like Red Rising treats advanced technology and knowledge — access is a function of power, not talent.

  • High Colors (Gold, and some Copper/Violet) — legal access to "High Magic": formal arcane traditions, taught in Gold institutions, tightly regulated and recorded.
  • Obsidian — magic is rare and typically granted, not learned; a Gold house may bind minor combat enchantments into an Obsidian's gear or body.
  • Gray — limited legal access, mostly utility/field magic issued as Society-sanctioned tools rather than personally mastered traditions.
  • Lower Colors — folk magic, hedge traditions, and black-market spells only; access to real arcane traditions is illegal.

Caste Traits (Racial-Style Templates)

Obsidian — bred for war, raised harsh

  • +2 Strength, +1 Constitution
  • Unbreaking: advantage on saves vs. exhaustion; resistance to damage from extreme environments (cold, heat, falling)
  • Bred for the Line: +1 to melee damage rolls, or advantage on Athletics checks to shove/grapple/climb
  • War Chant: bonus action to begin; Concentration. While chanting, the Obsidian and allies within 15 ft. are immune to frightened. At the start of each of the Obsidian's turns, every "weak" creature (CR 1 or lower) within 30 ft. that can hear it must succeed a Wisdom save (DC 8 + prof + Con mod) or become frightened until the Obsidian's next turn
  • Counts as one size category larger for carrying capacity/grapple purposes

Gray — quick, tough, pack hunters

  • +2 Dexterity, +1 Constitution
  • Pack Tactics: advantage on an attack roll against a creature if at least one other Gray/ally is within 5 ft of it
  • Tracker's Eye: advantage on Survival checks to track; proficiency in Perception if not already proficient
  • +2 bonus to Initiative

Gold — good at everything, elite breeding

  • +1 to two ability scores of choice, +1 Intelligence
  • Bred to Command: once per short rest, grant an ally within 30 ft advantage on their next attack roll, save, or check
  • Peerless Instinct: proficiency in one saving throw of choice

Ignores armor — ignore the AC bonus from worn armor (shields/magic bonuses still apply).

Iron Rain / Drop Pod Mechanics

Two pods launch during the hot/early Iron Rain (Act 1): Pod 1 carries the Gold + Obsidian PCs; Pod 2 carries the Gray Troupe Leader + Gray PCs. Both land in the same district of Dis but not adjacent.

  • Landing hazard: each PC makes a Dexterity save (DC 13) as their pod takes damage during the hot launch
  • Failure: pod lands off-target and rough — 2d6 bludgeoning damage, PC lands slightly separated from pod-mate(s)
  • Success: clean landing, no damage, group stays together

Zero-G Corridor (Act 1 escape puzzle)

Setup: both squads reach the corridor together. The ship shakes, hull gets cut into from outside, gravity fails. Roll initiative.

Round 1: the leechcraft carves fully through the hull. Flying devils (Barbtail Skirmishers) pour through first, followed by the Iron Ward Enforcer.

Mid-fight escalation: at a dramatic moment, the leechcraft tears free — a single round of vacuum pull with debris, not an ongoing hazard.

End condition: enemy dead, shuttle bay door opens.

  • Movement: no walking in zero-G. Push off a surface (Strength (Athletics), DC 12) to drift your speed in a straight line until you hit something or reach an anchor.
  • Anchors: fixed points placed like stepping stones through the corridor — these define the viable route.
  • Vacuum pull + debris (one round only): Strength save (DC 13) or pulled toward the opening; Dexterity save (DC 12) or 1d6 bludgeoning from debris.
  • Recoil: any weapon attack made while not braced against an anchor pushes the attacker 5 ft. directly away from their target after the attack resolves.
  • Enemies aren't slowed by zero-G the way PCs are — let them move freely.

Caste traits matter here: Obsidians push off harder and resist the vacuum pull more easily (Strength); Grays thread debris and stick anchor grabs more reliably (Dexterity).

Dis/Dispater — Plane-Conquest Reframing

  • Dis is not conquered territory — it is a long-standing ally. This one-shot's Iron Rain is not an occupation follow-up; it's the opening strike of a sudden, total betrayal.
  • The "too quiet" unease (Act 1): Dis has no idea the invasion is coming.
  • Guards "lighter than expected" (Act 2): Dis is staffed like an ally's city, not a warzone.
  • The Logbook: the cross-color breeding deals were quiet, sanctioned-but-secret arrangements between two allied hierarchies. The Society now wants the pipeline fully "in house" — no longer trusting Dis, and eliminating the risk of exposure.
  • Thematically, betrayal is the throughline: the Society betrays Dis, the Gray Troupe Leader betrays the Society, and the PCs may betray their orders — all in the same session.
