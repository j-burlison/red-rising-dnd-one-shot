# "Benediction of Dis" — Lore & Mechanics

## Oathbound Compulsion
Every operative — Obsidian and Gray alike — bears two sigils implanted at birth, one on each hand, binding them to obey the direct spoken orders of a designated superior (in this one-shot, the Gold commander).

**Trigger:** A PC takes an action that directly defies an order given by their bound superior.

**Each PC can break the oath twice per encounter — one sigil per break:**
- **1st break (right-hand sigil):** 2d6 psychic damage (Constitution save, DC 14, for half) + disadvantage on the next roll made immediately after
- **2nd break (left-hand sigil):** 2d6 psychic damage (Constitution save, DC 14, for half) + disadvantage on the next roll + **1 level of exhaustion**
- Once the right-hand sigil has broken, that PC's next defiant action automatically breaks the left-hand sigil too — no new order is required to trigger it
- **After both sigils are broken:** the PC is fully free of the compulsion for the rest of the encounter — further orders have no mechanical effect on them
- **Disabled sigils:** a Rising agent's sigils may already be dead, cut by a devil's pact or by the Rising (Irina's are). The compulsion has no effect on them, so each direct order becomes a choice: pretend to still be bound, or defy it openly and break cover

The compulsion is passive and always active — it doesn't require an action to resist, it simply triggers the moment defiance occurs. Players should know their oath is watching before Act 3, ideally reinforced narratively in Act 1 (a beat where an Obsidian visibly can't act against an order, or flinches when tempted to). Consider narrating the sigils visibly cracking/burning out on each break for a strong table moment.

## Caste-to-Class Mapping
Caste is a social/legal tier, not a species restriction — any race can belong to any Color. Caste determines training, starting gear, and legal access to magic. **Only Gray and Obsidian are playable castes in this one-shot** — Gold is reserved for Servian as an NPC commander.

**Gold** *(NPC-only in this one-shot)* — bred and trained to rule and command
- Paladin (Oath of Conquest/Glory fits the Society's ethos well; a defecting Gold could flip to Oath of Redemption)
- Fighter (Eldritch Knight) — battlefield commanders who blend martial and arcane
- Bard (College of Eloquence) — political/administrative Golds
- Wizard — the "Peerless Scarred" intellectual/strategist track

**Obsidian** — bred for war, raised for loyalty, bonded to a Gold house
- Barbarian — raw frontline power, Rage as controlled savagery
- Fighter (Battle Master or Champion) — disciplined shock troops
- **Sorcerer** — rare genetic anomaly, traced to illicit Gold-Obsidian bloodline mixing (draconic Gold houses have been known to splice dragon blood into Obsidian stock); the Society treats these as flaws or threats, not gifts, which adds tension to a caste already built on obedience
- *Thematic note:* Obsidians are the most affected by the Oathbound sigils narratively — their whole upbringing is obedience, so breaking it should feel like the biggest personal violation at the table

**Gray** — the Society's soldiers, police, and special forces
- Rogue (Soldier or Inquisitive-flavored) — infiltration, small-unit tactics
- Ranger — battlefield scouts, survivalists
- Fighter — standard-issue soldiers
- **Warlock** — a quiet pact made with a devil broker, for power, information, or survival; thematically sharp for this one-shot since it mirrors the exact crime the party investigates (Colors bargaining with devils)
- *Thematic note:* Grays are trained to question and observe, which is exactly why your Rising agent is a Gray — it's the caste most primed to notice cracks in the system

*(Copper, Violet, Green, Blue, Red, etc. — deferred to full campaign; not needed for this one-shot's limited caste roster.)*

## Magic Access by Caste
Magic in this setting is treated like Red Rising treats advanced technology and knowledge — access is a function of power, not talent.

- **High Colors (Gold, and some Copper/Violet)** — legal access to "High Magic": formal arcane traditions, taught in Gold institutions, tightly regulated and recorded. This is the magic of Wizards, high-tier Clerics/Paladins bound to Society-sanctioned faiths, and Bards trained in official colleges.
- **Obsidian** — magic is rare and typically *granted*, not learned — a Gold house may bind minor combat enchantments into an Obsidian's gear or body (explains magic weapons/armor on Obsidian PCs without breaking the "bred for war, not study" theme). Obsidians casting spells themselves should be an exception, not a norm.
- **Gray** — limited legal access, mostly utility/field magic (detection, communication, minor illusion) issued as Society-sanctioned tools rather than personally mastered traditions. A Gray Ranger/Rogue with a few spells reads as "issued gear," not personal power.
- **Lower Colors** — folk magic, hedge traditions, and black-market spells only; access to real arcane traditions is illegal, which is exactly why the Chimera Codex plot (cross-color breeding magic bought from devils) resonates — it's the same shape of crime as a lowColor buying illegal magic, just with higher stakes.

This gives you a built-in campaign tension for later: a Red or Copper with real magical talent is dangerous to the whole caste structure, mirroring Darrow's "impossible" potential in the source material.

## Caste Traits (Racial-Style Templates)
Caste layers mechanical traits on top of class, similar to a race/species template in standard 5e.

*Your Color's ability score increases and traits stack with your race's. You get both.*

**Obsidian — bred for war, raised harsh**
- +2 Strength, +1 Constitution
- **Unbreaking:** advantage on saves vs. exhaustion; resistance to damage from extreme environments (cold, heat, falling)
- **Bred for the Line:** +1 to melee damage rolls, or advantage on Athletics checks to shove/grapple/climb
- **War Chant:** bonus action to begin; requires Concentration (ends if the Obsidian takes damage and fails a Constitution save, DC 10 or half damage taken, whichever is higher, or if incapacitated). While chanting, the Obsidian and allies within 15 ft. are immune to the frightened condition. At the start of each of the Obsidian's turns while chanting, every "weak" creature (CR 1 or lower — covers Ash-Bound Wretches) within 30 ft. that can hear the chant must succeed on a Wisdom save (DC 8 + proficiency bonus + Constitution modifier) or become frightened of the Obsidian until the start of the Obsidian's next turn
- Counts as one size category larger for carrying capacity/grapple purposes

**Gray — quick, tough, pack hunters**
- +2 Dexterity, +1 Constitution
- **Pack Tactics:** advantage on an attack roll against a creature if at least one other Gray/ally is within 5 ft of it
- **Tracker's Eye:** advantage on Survival checks to track; proficiency in Perception if not already proficient
- +2 bonus to Initiative

**Gold — good at everything, elite breeding**
- +1 to two ability scores of choice, +1 Intelligence
- **Bred to Command:** once per short rest, grant an ally within 30 ft advantage on their next attack roll, save, or check
- **Peerless Instinct:** proficiency in one saving throw of choice

*(Subject to further balance adjustment — Gold package in particular may need tuning post-playtest.)*

## Weapons & Armor

*Every operative is issued all Universal gear: Duro-Steel Armor, Stem Injectors, a Pulse Blade, a Pulse Rifle, a Pulse Fist, and 2 Pulse Grenades.*

**Razor**
- Martial melee weapon, finesse, 1d8 slashing, light
- Against a razor, the target is treated as unarmored: its AC is what it would be without armor (10 + its Dexterity modifier, or its Unarmored Defense if it has that feature), plus any shield and magic bonuses. Worn armor and natural armor don't count.
- Neurally/thought-linked — requires attunement or a minimum Int/Dex to wield without penalty; thematically tied to Gold training, though any sufficiently trained PC could plausibly wield one (both the Gold and the Gray Troupe Leader carry one in the Act 3 climax, making both sides of the final fight equally lethal)
- Optional flavor rule: a natural 20 can sever a limb or disable a body part narratively — no mechanical stat needed, just table drama

**Whip Mode.** The razor instantaneously toggles between blade and whip form — switching costs an **item interaction** (free, doesn't use the action or bonus action). In whip form:
- **Reach 10 ft.** instead of 5 ft.
- **1d6 slashing** instead of 1d8 (the thinner whip-form edge trades power for range) — still treats the target as unarmored as normal
- **No longer light** — can't be used for a two-weapon fighting off-hand attack while in whip form
- As an alternative to dealing damage on a hit, the wielder can instead attempt to **disarm or trip** the target at range, using the attack roll in place of the normal contested check

**Pulse Fist (universal)**
- **3 charges per short rest** (including the planar jump); bonus action, 15-ft. cone, 2d6 force + push (DC 15 Dex)

**Pulse Armor (Gold)**
- Equivalent to Plate (base AC 18)
- **Pulse Shield** (built in): grants resistance to slashing damage from razors specifically — the in-fiction counter to a razor's unarmored-treatment trait
- Heavy armor, Strength requirement as normal

**Duro-Steel Armor (everyone else)**
- Maps directly to standard 5e armor types at equivalent AC (Light/Medium/Heavy = Leather/Chain/Plate equivalents)
- No special property — fully vulnerable to a razor's unarmored-treatment trait

**Stem Injectors (universal — all armor)**
- Built into every suit of armor in this one-shot, Duro-Steel and Pulse Armor alike
- Holds **3 charges per short rest**; each charge triggers as a **bonus action** and functions as a Greater Healing Potion (4d4+4 healing)
- Replaces the need to track consumable healing potions or rely on a dedicated healer — anyone can self-stabilize mid-fight
- An ally within 5 ft. can trigger a downed or incapacitated creature's injector as an action (one charge from the wearer's armor)

**Gravity Boots (Gold)**
- Magnetized boots standard-issue to Gold officers for shipboard and void operations
- Function mechanically like *Boots of Flying*: while active, the wearer has a flying speed equal to their normal speed and ignores zero-G push-off/anchor rules entirely, moving freely along any surface — floor, wall, or ceiling
- Limited-use like the source item (2 charges, recharging after a long rest) rather than unlimited — gives a natural point where they can run out or get damaged mid-fight
- Servian wears a pair into the Act 1 Zero-G Corridor fight (see his stat block for a DM note on having them run out of charges or take damage mid-encounter)

## Iron Rain / Drop Ship Mechanics
Both squads ride down together during the hot/early Iron Rain (Act 1): Servian, Ryn, and every PC in a single drop ship flown by a Blue pilot. On the way down they see another drop ship destroyed by a bolt of infernal magic and a Gold's starShell tackled midair by a massive winged devil, then a second winged devil spears the Blue through the cockpit window and the ship crash-lands. In the wreckage afterward is a good spot for Ryn to seed suspicion with the Grays, or for a command moment between Servian and the Obsidians.

**The planar jump is a short rest.** The jump from the Concordia to Dis only takes a few minutes, but time and space warp as the Concordia makes it, so the party gets the benefits of a short rest — Hit Dice, short-rest features, Stem Injectors (3), and Pulse Fists (3) all refill before landing. The crash landing below happens after the jump, on arrival.

- **Crash landing:** each PC makes a Dexterity save (DC 13) as the drop ship hits the ground
  - **Failure:** 2d6 bludgeoning damage, and the PC is thrown clear of the wreck, separated from the squad
  - **Success:** braces in time, no damage, climbs out with the group
- **Warlock beat:** as the drop ship falls into Dis, every warlock's patron speaks up in their head, angry but amused that the Society has come to strike Dis: *"The hubris, to come here and turn upon your ally."*

## Zero-G Corridor (Act 1 escape puzzle)

**Setup (pre-combat, narrated):** Both squads — Obsidians with the Gold, Grays with their troupe leader — reach the corridor together. The ship shakes. Everyone sees the hull start getting cut into from outside. Gravity fails. Roll initiative.

**Round 1:** The leechcraft carves fully through the hull. Flying devils (Barbtail Skirmishers) pour through first, followed by their commander, the Iron Ward Enforcer.

**Mid-fight escalation:** The leechcraft tears free from the hull when the first Enforcer dies, or at the start of round 4, whichever comes first (a ship countermeasure or a player reaching the leechcraft's exterior control panel can also trigger it earlier). This creates a **single round of vacuum pull with debris**, not an ongoing hazard: a sharp, dangerous spike rather than a constant drain.

**End condition:** The encounter ends when the enemy is dead and the shuttle bay door opens.

### Mechanics

- **Movement:** No walking in zero-G. To move, push off a surface (Strength (Athletics) check, DC 12) to drift your speed in a straight line until you hit something or reach an anchor point. Changing direction requires reaching a new anchor to push off again.
- **Anchors:** Fixed points (pipes, crates, support struts) placed through the corridor like stepping stones — these define the viable route.
- **Vacuum pull + debris (the one round the leechcraft tears free — first Enforcer death, or the start of round 4, whichever comes first):** everyone in the corridor makes a Strength save (DC 13) or is pulled toward the opening; on the same round, debris deals 1d6 bludgeoning to anyone who fails a Dexterity save (DC 12). This is a single, sharp spike, not ongoing — after this round, the breach seals or the pull dissipates (the leechcraft is gone, taking the worst of the suction with it).
- **Recoil:** Any weapon attack (melee or ranged) made while not braced against an anchor point pushes the attacker 5 ft directly away from their target immediately after the attack resolves. Bracing against an anchor when attacking cancels the recoil. This cuts both ways — a useful retreat or a shove straight into debris or the vacuum pull.
- **Adrift:** A creature left unbraced after a failed push-off, a recoil, or the vacuum pull is Adrift until the start of its next turn. Melee attacks against an Adrift creature have advantage. Devils are never Adrift.
- **Enemies:** Devils in this section (Barbtail Skirmishers, the Iron Ward Enforcer) aren't slowed by zero-G the way PCs are — let them move and reposition freely, making them feel more dangerous here than elsewhere in the one-shot.

Caste traits matter here: Obsidians push off harder and resist the vacuum pull more easily (Strength); Grays thread debris and stick anchor grabs more reliably (Dexterity).

## Dis/Dispater — Plane-Conquest Reframing
- **Dis is not conquered territory — it is a long-standing ally.** Dis and the Society allied early, bonded by their shared hierarchical structures, while the Society conquered most of the other planes. This one-shot's Iron Rain is not an occupation follow-up; it's the opening strike of a sudden, total betrayal.
- This reframes several existing beats:
  - **The "too quiet" unease (Act 1):** Dis has no idea the invasion is coming — the silence isn't resistance, it's a false sense of security about to be shattered.
  - **Guards "lighter than expected" (Act 2):** Dis isn't garrisoned for war. It's staffed like an ally's city, not a warzone — which is exactly why the Society is striking now, while defenses are down.
  - **The Logbook:** the cross-color breeding deals weren't black-market smuggling between strangers — they were quiet, sanctioned-but-secret arrangements between two allied hierarchies. Souls for the breeding magic were part of the alliance's original deal. Now that pipeline is a liability the Society wants to bring fully **"in house"** — no longer trusting or needing Dis as an external supplier, and eliminating the risk of the arrangement being exposed by an outside party. That's the real, cold motive behind the betrayal.
- Thematically, this makes **betrayal** the throughline of the entire one-shot: the Society betrays Dis, the Gray Troupe Leader betrays the Society, and the PCs may betray their orders — all in the same session.
